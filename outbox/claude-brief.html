5 · One Thing To Sound Smart About

Gold is printing records near $4,409 while the 10-year yield hits a two-year high — a textbook break of the gold/real-yield inverse relationship. Rising real yields are supposed to sink a non-yielding asset. That gold is climbing anyway tells you the marginal buyer isn't a yield-sensitive Western fund; it's central banks (China led Q1 2026 official purchases of 244 tonnes, the most since Q4 2024) plus record ETF inflows. The tell: gold is now trading as a sovereign-credit and currency-debasement hedge, not a rates instrument — so don't short it just because yields are up.

6 · Learning Nugget

The "Fed put" is the market's ingrained assumption that the Fed will ease to backstop falling stocks. A rate hike into a selloff — like the one priced for Wednesday — is that put being revoked, which is why an ordinarily routine 25bp move is landing as a regime shift rather than a footnote.
