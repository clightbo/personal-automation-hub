The Angle

September's split screen: the Nasdaq gained 1.9% while the Dow lost 4.3%. Leadership has narrowed to AI and chips, so one earnings report can move the whole tape. That is powerful on days like today, and fragile if the AI story stumbles or yields climb further.

One New Thing

Why do strong growth numbers hurt stocks? When the economy runs hot, markets expect the Fed to keep rates higher for longer, which pushes up Treasury yields. Higher yields raise the discount rate applied to future profits, which cuts the present value of stocks, especially growth names.
