5 · One Thing to Sound Smart About

The Edge

Microsoft's headline capex “cut” to ~$175B (from ~$190B) isn't spending discipline — it's largely accounting. Two levers: (1) they extended data-center useful life from 15→25 years, which lowers annual depreciation and flatters operating income (a >$10B first-year boost across hyperscalers); and (2) more future data-center leases shift from finance to operating leases — and operating leases don't count in capex. The cash obligation to secure power, sites and compute is unchanged; only the presentation moved. So the bulls cheering “capex discipline” are partly cheering a reclassification — a classic earnings-quality tell.

6 · Learning Nugget

Hawkish dissent. When FOMC members formally vote against the majority in favor of tighter policy (a hike or holding when cuts are expected). Because dissents are rare and public, multiple hawkish dissents are a strong signal the committee's reaction function is tilted toward fighting inflation — raising the bar for any near-term cut.
