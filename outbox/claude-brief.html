The Angle

Monday's open is a tug-of-war between two stories moving in opposite directions: a constructive U.S.-China trade extension and resilient corporate earnings on one side, and a reignited Iran/Hormuz standoff pushing oil and yields higher together on the other. Higher oil normally reinforces the case for a hawkish Fed, which is exactly the pressure yields are showing at a fresh 2007-high — meaning this week's PCE print carries more weight than usual as the tiebreaker between the two narratives.

Gold broke its own record streak this morning, falling about 3.2% even as a genuine geopolitical shock (the Iran rejection) hit the tape. That's notable because gold had been climbing steadily on safe-haven demand through the weekend — its reversal alongside a firmer dollar and rising real yields suggests last week's rally was carrying more momentum and diversification flow than durable safe-haven conviction, since the one thing that should have extended it (an actual escalation) didn't.
