6 · Learning Nugget

Chokepoint risk premium. Oil traders price a separate "war risk" premium on top of ordinary supply and demand whenever a shipping chokepoint — like the Strait of Hormuz or the Bab el-Mandeb strait near Yemen — is threatened, because insurers and shippers reroute or halt cargo well before any barrel is actually lost. That's why crude can jump on an attack that hits zero production, and fall back just as fast once shipping routes look safe again — the premium tracks perceived risk, not realized supply.
