Events — Why & What's Next

Soft jobs data lifts stocks, but not yields

Why nowSeptember payrolls and PCE inflation both came in softer than expected, which pushed rate-hike bets for the Oct. 27-28 meeting down to a long shot. Yet the 10-year stayed near 5.27%, so bonds have not fully bought the "Fed is done" story.

Watch forWhether equities can keep climbing if the 10-year holds above 5.25%. A push toward 5.35% would test the rally.

Fed minutes Wednesday could read hawkish

Why nowThe Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023, and Chair Warsh stressed that inflation remains too high. The meeting predated Friday's jobs report, so the minutes will reflect the more hawkish picture.

Watch forThe minutes on Wednesday, Oct. 7. A hawkish tone could cause a bond-market wobble even though an October hike is unlikely.

US-Iran standoff keeps oil near $100 Brent

Why nowEfforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the Middle East conflict remain the main driver of crude. Oil feeds directly into inflation expectations and therefore yields.

Watch forWhether Washington and Tehran resume talks, as the President has said he expects. Progress would pull oil and yields lower together; a breakdown would do the opposite.

Worth Noting

The Angle

With few data releases, the market is trading two variables: oil and yields. Stocks have shown they can rally on rate-hike relief, but a 5.27% 10-year and $100 Brent put a ceiling on valuations if either moves higher.

One New Thing

What are FOMC minutes? They are a detailed record of a Fed meeting, released three weeks later. They show how divided policymakers were and which risks they discussed, which can move markets even though the decision itself is already known.
