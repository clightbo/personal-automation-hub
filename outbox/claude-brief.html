Events — Why & What's Next

A jobs miss revives hopes the Fed will hold

Why nowSeptember payrolls rose just 29,000 versus a roughly 84,000 consensus, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%. With yields at multi-decade highs, markets had been pricing some chance of a Fed hike; a weak labor market lowers that odds and sent futures higher and yields down.

Watch forWhether the rally holds after the open. A weak jobs print can flip from "good for rates" to "bad for growth" if investors start to worry about a slowdown. The Fed meets Oct. 27-28.

Yields retreat from 24-year highs

Why nowThe 10-year yield eased to 5.24% Thursday and fell to about 5.18% after the jobs data, with the 30-year near 5.57%. Softer inflation data earlier in the week and the weak payrolls number together reduced pressure on bonds, which has been the main drag on stocks this month.

Watch forA sustained move below 5.2% would be a meaningful relief for valuations; a bounce back toward 5.3% would put the rally at risk.

Oil rebounds as US-Iran talks stall

Why nowWTI settled at $92.87 on Thursday, up $2.45, after talks between the US and Iran stalled. Crude near $90+ keeps headline inflation elevated and complicates the case for rate cuts even as the job market weakens.

Watch forAny progress on talks. A deal would likely drop oil and yields together; a breakdown would push crude back toward $95-100 and revive stagflation worries.

Worth Noting

The Angle

The market is now trading the "bad news is good news" trade: with yields near 24-year highs, weak data helps stocks because it lowers rate risk. That works until the data gets weak enough to threaten earnings, so the next few prints matter more than usual.

One New Thing

What is the "term premium"? It is the extra yield investors demand for holding a long-term bond instead of rolling short-term ones. When it rises, long yields climb even if the Fed does nothing, which is part of why the 10-year and 30-year have been so high.
