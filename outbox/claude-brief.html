5 · One Thing To Sound Smart About

This yield spike is low-quality. Yields can rise for a "good" reason (stronger growth) or a "bad" one (more supply + inflation risk). Wednesday's 20-year reopening tailed — stopping at 5.163% vs. a 5.158% when-issued, with below-average direct bidding and dealers absorbing the slack. That's soft domestic demand meeting a ballooning deficit, i.e. a term-premium story, not a growth story.

Contrarian read: bulls citing "yields up = economy strong" have it backwards here. A term-premium-led backup historically compresses P/E multiples even when earnings are fine — so the melt-up tape and the bond tape are telling different stories. Watch the 5%+ long end. (Wolf Street; Mariemont Capital)

6 · Learning Nugget

Term premium is the extra yield investors demand to hold a long-dated bond instead of rolling short T-bills — compensation for inflation and supply risk. When yields rise because of term premium (not because the Fed is expected to hike), it signals worry about deficits and inflation rather than optimism, and it tends to weigh on equity valuations.
