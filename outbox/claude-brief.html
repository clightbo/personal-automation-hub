One Thing To Sound Smart About

The real AI risk isn't capex — it's the depreciation wave nobody's expensing yet.

The big five hyperscalers spent ~$412B on capex in 2025 and are on track for ~$760B in 2026, yet plan to recognize only ~$211B of depreciation this year — leaving ~$549B of spending parked on balance sheets, not yet hitting the income statement. As those assets "go live" in 2026–2028, depreciation explodes: Alphabet's alone is modeled to climb from ~$21B (2025) to ~$78B (2029).

The twist: the capex mix has shifted to short-lived compute (~60% of spend, up from ~43% in 2022). If GPUs really last 3–4 years, not the 5–6 assumed, reported margins are being flattered today and will get squeezed exactly as the capex bill peaks. Debt is already filling the gap — up from 9% of capex (FY24) to 32% (LTM).

Sources: FactSet Insight, Epoch AI, Bank of England July FSR, Silicon Analysts.

Learning Nugget

Depreciation lag: capex is cash out the door today, but it hits earnings gradually over an asset's useful life. That timing gap is why a company can post record profits while its free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex) turns negative — exactly what's spooking investors about Alphabet and Tesla right now.
