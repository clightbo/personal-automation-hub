Friday closed out a rocky week on a strong note, but the picture has already shifted heading into Monday. On the fiscal side, the shutdown risk that has dogged Washington twice already this year is off the table for now: Congress passed, and Trump signed, a stopgap bill funding the government through December 11, pushing the next funding fight past the midterms. AI infrastructure deal flow remains the other live thread into Monday, with Akamai's $11.6 billion Anthropic compute deal and Microsoft's revamped, dividend-boosted Copilot still the freshest catalysts, and Nvidia holding its reclaimed market-cap lead over Apple by roughly $497 billion.

Events — Why & What's Next

Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz plan, putting Friday's oil relief in question

Why nowIran's foreign minister said Friday that Tehran had offered Washington a "concrete" seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear negotiations, in exchange for the U.S. lifting its naval blockade on Iranian ports, granting oil-sale waivers, and observing a ceasefire extending to Lebanon. Iran ruled out nuclear concessions, and over the weekend Trump rejected the proposal, with Secretary of State Rubio cautioning against calling the talks a breakthrough. The rejection undercuts the exact narrative — a reopening deal — that helped ease oil prices and lift stocks on Friday.

The Angle

Friday's rally leaned on two supports: a Fed that's paused rather than escalated, and hopes that Hormuz would reopen. The weekend knocked one of those supports out — Trump's rejection of Iran's proposal reintroduces the exact geopolitical risk oil had been pricing out. Combined with the Fed still holding roughly 71% odds of an October hike, Monday's open is a genuine test of whether Friday's optimism survives contact with the news, or whether both stories reverse together.

One New Thing

Gold and Bitcoin are moving in opposite directions even though both are usually read as risk barometers. Gold has pushed further into record territory near $4,300-4,320/oz on safe-haven demand, while Bitcoin has kept sliding to around $84,400, down about 2.4% on its latest move. That split — traditional safe-haven strength alongside crypto weakness — suggests the "risk-off" trade is being expressed selectively, not broadly, heading into a week with both a Fed data point and a live geopolitical flashpoint.
